Methodology

  • A fixed panel of synthetic personas is defined by explicit product attitudes (novelty seeking, brand loyalty, health concern, price sensitivity), grouped into segments.
  • Each persona receives one self-contained prompt containing the complete concept and every survey question, and returns one JSON object with all answers. No answer depends on conversational memory.
  • Numeric items use a 1-5 scale. Means and rating distributions are computed over valid responses only; failed responses are listed as warnings.
  • Representative quotes are the open-text answers of the respondent whose first rating is closest to their segment mean. They illustrate, they do not prove.
  • The run is reproducible from manifest.json: engine, model, seed, concept, panel, questions and exact prompts are recorded.

Warnings

  • Segment adventurous_foodie has a small sample (n=1); treat its means as anecdotal.
  • Segment mainstream_drinker has a small sample (n=1); treat its means as anecdotal.
  • Segment skeptical_health_conscious has a small sample (n=1); treat its means as anecdotal.

Limitations

  • These are simulated hypotheses, not human evidence. No real consumer was surveyed.
  • Synthetic personas reflect the prompt, the model and its training data; they can be confidently wrong, overly agreeable, and less varied than real people.
  • Rating levels are not calibrated to real purchase behavior; compare concepts against each other, never read a mean as a forecast.
  • Segments are defined by explicit product attitudes written by the study author, not measured populations.
  • Validate any promising signal with a human panel before making decisions.
